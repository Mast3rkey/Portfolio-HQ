## Business summary

Apple designs an integrated solution spanning hardware, operating systems,
software applications, and related services, and states that successful
competition depends on continuing product and technology innovation.
[AAPL-C04] FY2025 net sales were $416.2 billion, including $209.6 billion
from iPhone and $109.2 billion from Services. [AAPL-C01] Services revenue
increased 14% in FY2025 -- a materially faster growth rate than the
company overall -- while Greater China revenue declined, primarily because
of lower iPhone sales. [AAPL-C02] Apple spent $34.6 billion on research
and development in FY2025, approximately 8% of net sales. [AAPL-C03]

**Apple relies on a globally distributed, largely outsourced manufacturing
and supply base.** The company relies on outsourced manufacturing and
logistics, largely outside the U.S., and obtains some components from
single or limited sources. [AAPL-C05] Apple identifies Android, Windows,
game platforms, aggressive pricing, and imitation of product features as
important competitive pressures. [AAPL-C06]

**FY2026 Q2 results show continued growth acceleration.** Revenue was
$111.2 billion, up 17% year over year, and diluted EPS was $2.01, up 22%
-- a growth rate exceeding the FY2025 full-year pace. [AAPL-C07] Apple
reported more than $28 billion of Q2 operating cash flow, a new
installed-base record across major product categories and geographies, a
$0.27 dividend, and a new $100 billion repurchase authorization. [AAPL-C08]

**The evidence bundle's own explicit uncertainty judgment, carried forward
unresolved:** Apple remains exposed to product-cycle execution, global
supply-chain concentration, trade restrictions, regulatory and antitrust
action, developer and content relationships, currency, and consumer
demand. [AAPL-C09] This record treats that as an open, unresolved
condition, not a settled durable state.

## Investment thesis

**Concise statement:** Apple is an integrated hardware-software-services
ecosystem showing accelerating growth into FY2026 (17% revenue growth, 22%
EPS growth, a new installed-base record, and a new $100 billion repurchase
authorization), with Services now growing materially faster than the
company overall -- but the same evidence period shows a concurrent Greater
China revenue decline and a continued heavy reliance on outsourced,
geographically concentrated manufacturing.

**Expanded narrative:** Apple's FY2025 results show a large, diversified
revenue base ($416.2B net sales) with Services -- a higher-margin,
recurring-revenue category structurally distinct from hardware sales --
growing 14%, faster than the company as a whole [AAPL-C01, AAPL-C02]. That
same period shows a Greater China revenue decline attributed primarily to
lower iPhone sales [AAPL-C02] -- a reminder that Apple's largest single
hardware category and a major geography can move in different directions
within the same reporting period. The most recently reported quarter
(FY2026 Q2) shows this growth accelerating further: 17% revenue growth,
22% EPS growth, over $28 billion of operating cash flow, and a new
installed-base record across major product categories and geographies
[AAPL-C07, AAPL-C08] -- concrete, recent evidence that the integrated
ecosystem strategy [AAPL-C04] continues to translate into both growth and
substantial cash generation, alongside continued large-scale capital
return (dividend plus a new $100 billion repurchase authorization).

**Conditions required for the thesis to remain valid** (qualitative,
observable -- no arbitrary numerical threshold):
- Services revenue growth continues to outpace overall company growth, or
  at minimum does not reverse sharply
- Installed-base growth continues across major product categories and
  geographies
- Greater China revenue stabilizes or does not deteriorate further in a
  sustained way
- Operating cash flow and the capital-return program (dividend plus
  repurchase authorization) continue to be funded from operations without
  requiring incremental debt

**Thesis-weakening conditions** (analytical judgment, presented for
review -- observable and qualitative, deliberately without a specific
numerical trigger):
- A sustained reversal of Services revenue growth or a sustained
  deceleration relative to the overall company growth rate
- A sustained (not single-quarter) further decline in Greater China
  revenue
- Evidence of a material, sustained iPhone demand deceleration, given its
  continued roughly-half share of total net sales
- A material adverse regulatory or antitrust development affecting the
  integrated hardware-software-services ecosystem or developer/content
  relationships

## Capital priority and next-dollar considerations

**Business quality**, per the evidence above, shows a large-scale,
integrated hardware-software-services ecosystem with accelerating recent
growth, strong cash generation, and expanding shareholder capital return,
offset by a concurrent Greater China revenue decline, continued heavy
iPhone revenue concentration, and structural supply-chain and regulatory
exposures.

**Capital priority is a separate question, and AAPL's own current T2
placement (1.65% target) is itself a data point this record preserves as
historical policy, not evidence.** Whether the next investment dollar is
better spent on AAPL than on another T2 name or another governed holding
entirely is not settled by this record: this record does not perform a
cross-holding capital-priority ranking, and no numerical score or
composite index is produced. **What can be said qualitatively:** AAPL's
evidence base -- accelerating growth, a materially faster-growing Services
segment, and substantial cash generation -- is comparably strong to the
highest-conviction records elsewhere in this corpus, but the concurrent
Greater China decline and continued iPhone concentration are real,
disclosed limitations on treating the recent acceleration as unambiguously
durable. **What would be lost if AAPL were absent:** the portfolio's only
direct exposure to a large-scale, vertically-integrated consumer-hardware-
and-services ecosystem with a recurring, higher-margin Services revenue
stream distinct from every other T2 holding in this batch.

**Factors that could support greater capital priority** include AAPL's
accelerating recent growth, its faster-growing Services segment, and its
substantial cash generation and capital-return program. **Factors favoring
an alternative use of capital** include the concurrent Greater China
revenue decline, continued heavy iPhone revenue concentration, and the
structural supply-chain/regulatory risks the company's own filing names.
A reviewer weighting AAPL's recent growth acceleration and Services
momentum more heavily could reasonably favor greater priority; a reviewer
weighting the China/iPhone-concentration risk more heavily could
reasonably favor less. **This record does not resolve that question and
does not rank AAPL against any other holding.**

Business quality and portfolio capital priority are separate judgments.
This record does not determine the governed target: AAPL's current T2
tier and 1.65% target remain binding, this Intelligence record does not
change them, and a final next-dollar decision requires portfolio-wide
comparison and principal approval.
