## Business summary

Constellation Energy, following the January 2026 acquisition of Calpine,
describes itself as the largest private-sector power producer globally and
the largest U.S. nuclear-energy company, with approximately 55 GW of
capacity and about 2.5 million customer accounts -- a company-described
scale claim, treated as an issuer statement rather than an independently
verified market-share figure. [CEG-C01] The Calpine acquisition added
approximately 23 GW across 72 generation and battery-storage assets and
about 62 TWh of annual retail load. [CEG-C02] At 2025 year-end, pre-Calpine
owned generation totaled 31,676 MW and contracted generation totaled 4,798
MW. [CEG-C03]

**The core asset is a large, high-utilization nuclear fleet.** 2025 nuclear
fleet capacity factor was 94.7%, up marginally from 94.6% in 2024 --
consistent, near-ceiling utilization of baseload nuclear capacity. [CEG-C04]
A 20-year power purchase agreement with Microsoft supports the planned
restart of the 835 MW Crane Clean Energy Center, subject to regulatory and
execution conditions -- long-duration offtake demand explicitly tied to
data-center/AI electricity demand, though the restart itself is not yet
complete. [CEG-C05]

**Q1 2026 results show continued operational execution and affirmed
guidance, alongside a large GAAP-vs-adjusted gap.** GAAP earnings were $4.49
per share and adjusted operating earnings were $2.74 per share; management
affirmed adjusted operating earnings guidance of $11 to $12 per share for
the full year. [CEG-C06] Nuclear operations produced 40 TWh at a 92.3%
capacity factor in the quarter, with two refueling outages averaging 23
days. [CEG-C07] Constellation reported roughly $335 million of
year-to-date share repurchases and $4.7 billion of remaining authorization
in its Q1 2026 presentation. [CEG-C08]

**The evidence bundle's own explicit uncertainty judgment, carried forward
unresolved:** investment outcomes remain exposed to plant availability,
nuclear regulation, power and fuel prices, customer contracting, Calpine
integration, and the capital required for growth and life extension.
[CEG-C09] This record treats that as an open, unresolved condition, not a
settled durable state.

## Investment thesis

**Concise statement:** Constellation is a scale nuclear/multi-fuel power
producer with a large, well-utilized nuclear fleet and a long-duration,
AI-demand-linked offtake contract, whose evidence base shows continued
operational execution and affirmed guidance in the most recent quarter --
but whose business quality must be weighed against real integration risk
from a large, recent acquisition and structural exposure to power-market
conditions.

**Expanded narrative:** The Calpine acquisition materially changed CEG's
scale and generation mix in January 2026 -- a large, recent integration
whose full operational and financial track record does not yet exist in
this evidence base [CEG-C01, CEG-C02]. The pre-existing nuclear fleet
continues to run at a very high capacity factor (94.7% in 2025, 92.3% in
Q1 2026) [CEG-C04, CEG-C07], and the Crane Clean Energy Center
restart -- backed by a 20-year Microsoft PPA -- represents a specific,
evidenced example of data-center-driven demand for clean-firm power
[CEG-C05], though its completion remains subject to regulatory and
execution conditions, not yet realized. Q1 2026 guidance was affirmed
[CEG-C06], a positive signal, but the same quarter's GAAP EPS diverged
substantially from the adjusted operating metric management guides to --
a data point this record flags for continued monitoring rather than
either dismissing or over-weighting from a single quarter.

**Conditions required for the thesis to remain valid** (qualitative,
observable -- no arbitrary numerical threshold):
- Nuclear fleet capacity factor remains at or near its historical
  94%+ range
- Full-year adjusted operating EPS guidance ($11-$12/share) is met or
  reaffirmed in subsequent quarters
- Calpine integration proceeds without a material operational or financial
  disruption
- The Crane Clean Energy Center restart proceeds toward completion under
  its disclosed regulatory and execution conditions

**Thesis-weakening conditions** (analytical judgment, presented for
review -- observable and qualitative, deliberately without a specific
numerical trigger):
- A sustained decline in nuclear fleet capacity factor below its
  historical range
- A withdrawal, reduction, or material downward revision of full-year
  adjusted operating EPS guidance
- Evidence of material Calpine integration difficulty (cost overruns,
  operational disruption, customer or regulatory friction)
- A material adverse regulatory or execution development affecting the
  Crane Clean Energy Center restart

## Capital priority and next-dollar considerations

**Business quality**, per the evidence above, shows a scale nuclear/
multi-fuel power producer with a genuinely scarce, high-utilization nuclear
asset base and a specific, evidenced example of AI/data-center-linked
long-duration demand, offset by real, current integration risk from a
large recent acquisition and structural exposure to power-market and
regulatory conditions.

**Capital priority is a separate question, and CEG's own current T2
placement (1.65% target) is itself a data point this record preserves as
historical policy, not evidence.** Whether the next investment dollar is
better spent on CEG than on another T2 name or another governed holding
entirely is not settled by this record: this record does not perform a
cross-holding capital-priority ranking, and no numerical score or
composite index is produced. **What can be said qualitatively:** CEG's
nuclear-fleet utilization and AI-demand-linked offtake evidence support a
real, differentiated thesis, but the evidence base's silence on
balance-sheet leverage and the recency of the Calpine integration are real,
disclosed limitations on how confidently this record can compare CEG's
financial risk to other governed holdings' own more fully evidenced
records. **What would be lost if CEG were absent:** the portfolio's only
direct nuclear/merchant-power-generation exposure and its only direct,
contracted example of data-center electricity demand translating into a
specific long-duration utility offtake agreement.

**Factors that could support greater capital priority** include CEG's
scarce nuclear asset base, its affirmed guidance, and its specific,
contracted AI-demand-linked offtake agreement. **Factors favoring an
alternative use of capital** include the unresolved Calpine integration
risk, the Q1 2026 GAAP-vs-adjusted-EPS divergence, and the absence of any
leverage disclosure in this record's evidence base. A reviewer weighting
CEG's nuclear scarcity and AI-demand linkage more heavily could reasonably
favor greater priority; a reviewer weighting the integration and
leverage-evidence gaps more heavily could reasonably favor less. **This
record does not resolve that question and does not rank CEG against any
other holding.**

Business quality and portfolio capital priority are separate judgments.
This record does not determine the governed target: CEG's current T2
tier and 1.65% target remain binding, this Intelligence record does not
