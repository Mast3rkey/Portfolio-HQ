## Business summary

Oracle combines database licensing and support, enterprise applications
(Fusion, NetSuite), and Oracle Cloud Infrastructure (OCI). The OCI buildout
materially changes the company's historical asset-light software profile --
this is not the same business, financially, as legacy Oracle database
licensing, and SaaS applications, support, and OCI margins should not be
treated as interchangeable in this record or in any comparison with peers.

## Backlog scale and quality

Oracle reported remaining performance obligations (RPO) of **$552.6
billion** at 2026-02-28, with approximately **12% expected to be recognized
in the following 12 months**, driven by significant cloud contracts. This
is an extraordinarily large disclosed backlog relative to the rest of this
batch -- but it is **long-dated and concentrated in significant
contracts**, explicitly **not equivalent to near-term revenue, profit, or
cash**. Counterparty concentration, capacity timing, and cancellation terms
are not fully disclosed in the inspected sources.

## Capital intensity and the AI/cloud infrastructure buildout

Oracle's first-nine-months fiscal 2026 capital expenditures reached **$39.2
billion**, versus **$12.1 billion** a year earlier -- primarily for
data-center expansion -- and management expected the upward trend to
continue. This is current, filed evidence of an aggressive,
capacity-driven buildout consistent with the disclosed cloud-contract
backlog, but it is management expectation for the future, not historical
fact, and returns depend on contract conversion and utilization that are
not yet established.

## Financing and balance-sheet resilience

Oracle financed this expansion with **substantial senior-note issuance and
mandatory convertible preferred stock**, while continuing dividends. This
makes leverage, dilution, and refinancing central to Oracle's capital
allocation in a way not shared by the batch's more asset-light software and
cybersecurity names. Operating cash flow, cash balances, and contracted
demand may support the financing strategy, but the ultimate burden depends
on future capex, equity issuance, and contract cash conversion.

## Enterprise applications -- Fusion and NetSuite

This record's evidence base distinguishes Oracle's enterprise-applications
business (Fusion, NetSuite) from its infrastructure (OCI) business in the
filings, but does not establish segment-level unit economics precise enough
to separate Fusion/NetSuite economics from OCI's capital-intensive profile
at the claim level this record relies on -- a disclosed limitation of
current segment-disclosure quality, not a finding either way.

## Competitors, substitutes, and shared drivers

Hyperscale cloud infrastructure providers are Oracle's primary named
competitive constraint on the OCI buildout specifically; database and
enterprise-application competitors constrain the licensing and Fusion/
NetSuite businesses separately. See
`BATCH8_ENTERPRISE_SOFTWARE_CYBERSECURITY_COMPARISON.md` for Oracle's
distinctly capital-intensive position relative to the batch's five other,
comparatively asset-light companies.

## Disconfirming evidence

- Extraordinary RPO ($552.6 billion) is long-dated and contract-concentrated
  -- not near-term revenue, profit, or cash.
- Capital expenditures and financing (new debt, mandatory convertible
  preferred stock) have risen sharply alongside the buildout.
- Backlog conversion, OCI capacity utilization, and infrastructure returns
  remain uncertain in the inspected sources.

## Thesis-break conditions

A thesis-break condition would include failure to convert large cloud RPO
into profitable revenue while capital expenditures, debt service, and
dilution remain structurally elevated. Strong utilization, pricing, and
contract cash conversion could instead produce durable infrastructure
returns -- this condition requires multi-year capacity, margin, and
cash-conversion evidence, not a single quarter.

## Unresolved questions

- Customer concentration within large cloud contracts.
- OCI capacity utilization and unit economics.
- Cerner integration economics.
- Post-year-end debt, equity, and capital-expenditure commitments.

## Capital priority and next-dollar considerations

**Business quality**, per the evidence above, shows an extraordinarily
large, currently growing disclosed cloud-contract backlog and a real,
aggressive infrastructure buildout aligned with that demand, set against
the batch's most acute capital-intensity, leverage, and dilution profile.

**Capital priority is a separate question, and Oracle's own current band
placement (0.75% target, 1.25x cap) -- shared with IBM, NOW, CRM, CRWD, and
PANW in this batch -- is itself a data point this record preserves as
historical policy, not evidence.** Oracle's distinct exposure within this
batch is the only name whose growth thesis is directly tied to large-scale,
debt- and equity-financed physical infrastructure investment -- a
fundamentally different capital-allocation mechanism from every other
company in this batch, all of which are comparatively asset-light. **What
would be lost if ORCL were absent:** exposure to the batch's only
capital-intensive, infrastructure-buildout-driven AI/cloud growth thesis,
and to its largest disclosed forward-revenue backlog.

**Factors that could support greater capital priority** include the sheer
scale of disclosed cloud-contract demand (RPO) and the real, current
capital commitment to serve it. **Factors favoring an alternative use of
capital** include the sharply elevated debt and dilution used to finance
that commitment, the batch's own required caution that Oracle's RPO is
long-dated and not near-term cash, and the absence, in the inspected
sources, of established OCI capacity-utilization or unit-economics
evidence that would confirm the buildout's return. A reviewer preferring
lower balance-sheet and financing risk, or more asset-light recurring-
revenue economics, could reasonably favor one of the batch's other five
companies instead.

Business quality and portfolio capital priority are separate judgments.
This record does not determine the governed target: Oracle's current band
tier and 0.75% target remain binding, this Intelligence record does not
change them, and a final next-dollar decision requires portfolio-wide
comparison and principal approval.
