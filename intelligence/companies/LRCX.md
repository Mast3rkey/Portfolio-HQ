## Business summary

Lam Research primarily supplies plasma etch and thin-film deposition
equipment, with particular strength in high-aspect-ratio etch for 3D NAND
memory fabrication — making Lam the most memory-concentrated company in
this four-company batch. Also operates the **Customer Support Business
Group (CSBG)**, recurring service/spares/upgrades revenue, which reached a
record $2.11B in Q3 FY2026 (+25% YoY), crossing $2B in a single quarter for
the first time.

Q3 FY2026 (quarter ended 2026-03-29, reported 2026-04-22): revenue
$5,841M (+9% QoQ, +24% YoY), GAAP gross margin 49.8%, GAAP net income
$1,825M ($1.45 diluted EPS). FY2025 (fiscal year ended 2025-06-29): revenue
$18.44B (+23.7% YoY, see Source-access disclosure for a conflicting figure
found), gross margin 48.7%. Gross margin has trended up consistently
across independent sources: 47.3% (FY2024) -> 48.7% (FY2025) -> 49.8-50.6%
across FY2026 quarters reported so far.

A Q2 FY2026 systems-revenue split cited in secondary sources (not verified
against a primary transcript) put Foundry at 59%, Memory (DRAM) at 34%
(23%), and NAND/Flash at 11% of systems revenue — this specific split
should be treated as approximate; it is the best available figure found in
this research pass but was not corroborated to primary-source confidence.

## Investment thesis (business-quality description, not a recommendation)

**Concise statement:** Lam Research is a leading etch/deposition equipment
supplier with particular strength in high-aspect-ratio NAND processes and
a growing recurring-service business — but it carries this batch's most
concentrated memory-cycle exposure, and the 2022-2023 downturn provides
direct, quantified evidence of how severe that concentration can be when
memory capex genuinely contracts.

**Expanded narrative:** Lam's technical strength in NAND etch (the Vantex
cryogenic system reportedly won a "key multi-generation etch decision at a
major NAND customer") and its growing CSBG recurring-revenue base (a
management target of >1.5x growth by calendar 2028 versus calendar 2024)
are real structural positives found in this research. Set against that:
the 2022-2023 memory/WFE downturn is the single sharpest downturn evidence
found for any company in this batch — a documented 31.4% year-over-year
revenue decline (Q1 FY2024, quarter ended 2023-09-24, versus Q1 FY2023,
quarter ended 2022-09-25 — distinct from two separate sequential 26.7% and
17.1% quarter-over-quarter declines in the two preceding quarters, see
Risks below) and a ~7%-of-workforce layoff (Form 8-K filed 2023-01-23;
CEO commentary on the 2023-01-25 earnings call cited both declining demand
and export-control effects — a mixed, company-stated cause, not a single
attribution this record asserts independently). One
secondary source frames Lam's relative valuation discount versus peers as
reflecting the market's own caution about this specific concentration —
i.e., the market itself appears to price Lam as carrying more concentrated
cyclical risk than its peers, consistent with (though not proof of) this
record's own reading of the evidence. China export-control exposure is
also material and currently escalating: a 2026 "affiliate rule"
restriction is company-quantified at approximately $600M for calendar
2026, on top of the 2022 action's disclosed ~13%-of-FY2022-revenue
estimated impact, with China's revenue share guided from a 43% Q1 FY2026
peak down to below 30% for calendar 2026.

**Conditions relevant to monitoring this thesis** (qualitative, observable):
- Q4 FY2026 results (2026-07-29, immediately following this record's
  cutoff) against company guidance ($6.6B +/- $400M revenue, $1.65 +/-
  $0.15 EPS)
- Memory-segment demand continuing at levels consistent with recent growth
  rather than re-entering a 2022-2023-style contraction
- China revenue share stabilizing near or below the guided sub-30% level
  for calendar 2026 rather than deteriorating further
- CSBG recurring revenue continuing its growth trajectory toward the
  >1.5x-by-2028 management target

**Conditions that would weaken confidence in the above:**
- A confirmed re-acceleration of memory-capex contraction resembling the
  2022-2023 episode's magnitude
- A further, larger China export-control action beyond the disclosed 2026
  "affiliate rule" impact
- A material guidance miss or cut at the 2026-07-29 Q4 FY2026 release
- Evidence of competitive share loss to Applied Materials in overlapping
  deposition/etch markets (see batch comparison artifact — AMAT and Lam
  are described in secondary sources as the two leading deposition/etch
  players, with AMAT reportedly leading deposition and Lam leading etch)

**The 2022-2023 memory downturn, precisely sequenced by quarter (the
clearest and most severe downturn episode found anywhere in this batch's
research — remediation pass, 2026-07-25):**

| Quarter | Period ended | Revenue | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 FY2023 | 2022-09-25 | $5,074M | — |
| Q2 FY2023 | 2022-12-25 | $5,278M | cycle peak |
| Q3 FY2023 | 2023-03-26 | $3,870M | -26.7% QoQ |
| Q4 FY2023 | 2023-06-25 | $3,207M | -17.1% QoQ |
| Q1 FY2024 | 2023-09-24 | $3,482M | +8.6% QoQ; **-31.4% YoY vs. Q1 FY2023** |

The often-cited "~31% decline" is the **Q1 FY2024-vs-Q1 FY2023
year-over-year comparison specifically** — it is not the same figure as,
and should not be conflated with, the two sequential quarter-over-quarter
declines (26.7% and 17.1%) that occurred in the two quarters immediately
before it. Lam forecast 2023 WFE spending would fall more than 20%
industry-wide, driven by memory-customer capex destocking; guidance for
the Q3 FY2023 quarter was cut from a prior $3.8B to a $2.8-3.4B range —
roughly -20% quarter-over-quarter at the midpoint, consistent with the
26.7% QoQ decline that quarter actually realized. Q1 FY2024's -31.4% YoY
revenue decline (adjusted EPS -34% YoY) is the single sharpest quarterly
decline figure found for any company in this batch.

Separately: on **2023-01-23**, Lam filed a Form 8-K (SEC EDGAR accession
0000707549-23-000005) disclosing a restructuring plan; CEO Tim Archer
publicly discussed a reduction of approximately 1,300 employee positions
(approximately 7% of total workforce) plus approximately 1,400
temporary-worker positions on the **2023-01-25** Q2 FY2023 earnings call
(quarter ended 2022-12-25) — that commentary cited both slowing
wafer-fabrication-equipment demand and the effect of the most recent US
export-control regulations as reasons, a mixed, company-stated causation
this record retains as stated rather than simplifying to a single cause.
The restructuring plan was expected to be substantially completed by the
end of the quarter ending 2023-06-25 (Q4 FY2023, the quarter that realized
the -17.1% QoQ decline in the table above).

**Export-control shock timeline (dated instances):**
- **October 2022** — following the Biden administration's BIS action, Lam
  estimated it would lose as much as $2.5B, approximately 13% of FY2022
  revenue.
- **2023 (exact date not confirmed — distinct from the 2023-01-23
  workforce-reduction 8-K above)** — a secondary source states Lam lost
  the ability to service certain already-installed China equipment
  following 2023 restrictions, with AMEC (a Chinese competitor) reportedly
  developing replacement parts (electrostatic chucks) to fill the
  resulting servicing gap for Lam's customers in China — notable because it
  implies a competitive-erosion consequence flowing directly from an
  export-control action, not merely a lost-sale consequence.
- **2026 "affiliate rule" (exact announcement date not confirmed)** —
  company-quantified impact of approximately $200M in the December 2025
  quarter and approximately $600M for calendar 2026; China revenue share
  guided from a 43% Q1 FY2026 peak (quarter ended ~September 2025) down to
  below 30% for calendar 2026.

## Margin-relevance evidence (factual, advisory only — no leverage recommendation)

Per `PI-0023` SSB.16 and `OPS-0006` SS4's Milestone 3 requirement.

- **Cyclicality**: the 2022-2023 memory downturn (detailed above) is the
  single most severe, best-quantified downturn episode found for any
  company in this four-company batch — a 31.4% YoY revenue decline
  (Q1 FY2024 vs. Q1 FY2023, distinct from the cycle's two sequential QoQ
  declines) and a ~7%-workforce layoff (officially filed 2023-01-23). A
  2019 memory downturn also affected
  Lam (industry DRAM revenue -37.5%, NAND -23.1%), with Lam's own
  non-GAAP EPS declining from $17.44 (calendar 2018) to $14.51 (calendar
  2019), roughly -16.8%, though calendar 2019 was still characterized as
  "the second-best year in Lam's history" — secondary sources attribute
  this relative resilience partly to diversification beyond memory into
  logic/foundry and CSBG growth, though this specific attribution was not
  independently verified.
- **Stock-price drawdown**: calendar-year 2022 full-year return reported
  as approximately -40.72% (split-adjusted for Lam's 2024 10-for-1 split),
  with an implied intra-year high-to-low decline of approximately 56% —
  moderate confidence, source attribution not fully pinned down. No
  primary-sourced peak-to-trough figure specifically for the 2022-2023
  memory-downturn stock price (as distinct from the full-calendar-year
  figure) was found — a genuine gap. Multiple single-day declines of 6-10%
  occurred in 2026 (e.g. -9.90% on 2026-06-23, -6.34% on 2026-07-15) tied
  to China export-curb news and sector-wide selling — single-day moves, not
  comparable to the 2022-2023 multi-quarter cyclical drawdown, and
  explicitly not conflated with it in this record.
- **Balance-sheet strength**: cash and equivalents (including restricted
  cash) approximately $4.77B as of 2026-03-29; long-term debt approximately
  $3.73B as of 2025-09-30 (a March-2026-current figure was not confirmed).
  Strong free cash flow ($3.62B over the first 9 months of FY2026) and a
  stated policy of returning at least 85% of free cash flow to
  shareholders suggest management prioritizes capital return over debt
  paydown; cash appears to exceed identified long-term debt by a wide
  margin based on the figures found, directionally suggesting low
  net-leverage risk, though this was not confirmed against a complete,
  current balance sheet (current/short-term borrowings were not itemized
  in sources found). No refinancing-specific disclosures (maturity
  schedule, credit ratings) were found.
- **Semiconductor-capex sensitivity, with a memory-specific amplifier**:
  Lam's exposure spans foundry/logic and memory, but its memory weighting
  (reportedly ~34% of Q2 FY2026 systems revenue, DRAM+NAND combined,
  unverified) is understood to be the largest of any company in this
  batch, and memory capex is the most volatile sub-segment of total WFE
  spending per the framing this task's own instructions and this
  repository's targets.yaml documentation already apply.
- **Export-control/geopolitical exposure**: material and currently
  escalating (2026 "affiliate rule," ~$600M calendar-2026 disclosed
  impact), on top of the largest single disclosed-percentage historical
  impact found in this batch (the 2022 action's estimated ~13%-of-FY2022-
  revenue impact).
- **Customer concentration**: China revenue swung from 34% (FY2025 full
  year) to a 43% peak (Q1 FY2026) to a guided sub-30% level (calendar
  2026) — the largest disclosed single-period swing found for any company
  in this batch, consistent with (though not proof of) lumpy,
  license-timing-driven shipment recognition rather than a smooth trend.
  Named individual top-customer percentages were not found.
- **Simultaneous-loss / correlated-risk with the rest of this batch**: KLA
  is described in one secondary source as having a services/inspection
  revenue mix "largely insensitive to WFE cyclicality" (a stability buffer
  KLAC's own record discusses), which Lam's CSBG may provide to a lesser
  degree on a percentage-of-revenue basis — a rough approximation from this
  record's own figures (FY2025 CSBG $7.2B against FY2025 total revenue
  $18.44B implies roughly 39% of revenue, though this depends on the
  $18.44B figure being correct given the unresolved conflict noted above).
  No source found in this research directly and quantitatively compares
  Lam's cyclical timing (lead/lag) against AMAT's or KLAC's — only
  amplitude evidence (Lam swings more sharply) was found, not phase/timing
  evidence. This distinction is stated explicitly for the batch comparison
  artifact rather than conflated into a single claim.
- **Detectability of thesis deterioration**: the 2022-2023 episode shows
  deterioration became visible via guidance cuts and realized revenue
  declines within roughly one to two quarters of the underlying memory-
  capex contraction beginning — a relatively fast detection window by
  guidance/earnings, though this record did not independently verify
  whether stock-price deterioration led or lagged that guidance-cut
  timeline (the specific 2022-2023 stock-drawdown-timing gap noted above).

## What exposure would be lost if LRCX were absent from the portfolio

Lam is the only company in this batch with concentrated, disclosed
strength specifically in memory (NAND/DRAM) fabrication equipment.
Removing Lam would shift the portfolio's semiconductor-equipment exposure
more heavily toward AMAT's broader-but-shallower-in-memory coverage,
KLAC's process-control specialization (largely orthogonal to memory-cycle
timing), and ASML's lithography exclusivity — none of which replicates
Lam's specific high-aspect-ratio NAND etch/deposition position or its
correspondingly higher memory-cycle amplitude.
