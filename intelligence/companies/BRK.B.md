## Business summary

Berkshire Hathaway is a decentralized conglomerate spanning insurance and
reinsurance, railroad, utilities and energy, manufacturing, service, and
retail businesses. [BRK-C01] The evidence base most fully documents the
insurance segment, which is also the segment structurally central to
Berkshire's capital-allocation model.

**Insurance float is large, growing, and currently carried at a negative
cost.** Insurance float was approximately $176.9 billion at 2026-03-31, up
about $500 million from 2025 year-end. [BRK-C02] Berkshire's combined
insurance operations generated underwriting profits in both Q1 2026 and Q1
2025, producing a negative average cost of float in both periods --
Berkshire is effectively paid to hold this capital, rather than paying a
funding cost for it, in the periods evidenced. [BRK-C03]

**Balance-sheet liquidity is exceptional.** Insurance businesses held $236.8
billion of cash, cash equivalents, and U.S. Treasury bills at 2026-03-31,
[BRK-C04] and separately held approximately $285.0 billion of equity
securities at the same date. [BRK-C05] Without prior regulatory approval,
Berkshire's principal insurance subsidiaries could declare approximately
$33 billion of ordinary dividends during 2026 [BRK-C08] -- a disclosed
figure indicating substantial intra-group capital mobility.

**Reserve and goodwill exposures are real and disclosed at scale.**
Estimated unpaid-loss and loss-adjustment-expense liabilities were $151.9
billion at 2026-03-31, and Berkshire's own filing warns that small
percentage changes in these estimates can materially affect earnings.
[BRK-C06] Goodwill was $83.2 billion and indefinite-lived intangible assets
were $19.0 billion at 2026-03-31, with four reporting units carrying less
than a 20% fair-value cushion in the prior annual review. [BRK-C07]

**The evidence bundle's own explicit uncertainty judgment, carried forward
unresolved:** Berkshire's reported results remain sensitive to catastrophe
losses, reserve estimation, regulated railroad and utility economics,
acquisition discipline, equity-market movements, and succession execution.
[BRK-C09] This record treats that as an open, unresolved condition, not a
settled durable state.

## Investment thesis

**Concise statement:** Berkshire combines a large, currently negative-cost
insurance float, exceptional balance-sheet liquidity, and a decentralized
group of largely uncorrelated operating businesses -- a structurally
advantaged capital-allocation platform whose scale and liquidity this
record's evidence base finds unusually well-documented, offset by real,
disclosed reserve-estimation, goodwill-impairment, and succession-execution
risk.

**Expanded narrative:** The insurance float -- ~$176.9B and growing, carried
at a negative cost across both compared quarters -- is the mechanical core
of Berkshire's capital-allocation advantage: it functions as investable
capital that costs less than nothing to hold, in the periods evidenced
[BRK-C02, BRK-C03]. That float sits alongside $236.8B of cash/Treasury-bill
liquidity and ~$285.0B of equity holdings [BRK-C04, BRK-C05], giving the
group substantial optionality to deploy capital opportunistically across
its insurance, railroad, utility, and industrial/retail operating
businesses. The evidence base does not itself detail the non-insurance
segments' individual operating economics, so this record's quality
assessment leans most heavily on the insurance/liquidity picture, which is
exceptionally strong, while disclosing that gap rather than assuming the
other segments share the same strength without direct evidence.

**Conditions required for the thesis to remain valid** (qualitative,
observable -- no arbitrary numerical threshold):
- Combined insurance underwriting results remain profitable, or at minimum
  the cost of float does not turn structurally and durably positive
- No reserve-estimate revision produces a disproportionate earnings impact
  relative to the scale Berkshire's own filing already flags as material
- No goodwill or intangible-asset impairment materializes in the
  previously-flagged thin-cushion reporting units
- Capital allocation (acquisitions, buybacks, or dividends from
  subsidiaries) continues to reflect the same disciplined pattern the
  evidence base describes rather than a departure from it

**Thesis-weakening conditions** (analytical judgment, presented for
review -- observable and qualitative, deliberately without a specific
numerical trigger):
- A sustained combined-ratio deterioration turning the cost of float
  structurally positive
- A material adverse reserve development beyond ordinary-course revision
- A goodwill or intangible-asset impairment in one of the four previously
  disclosed thin-cushion reporting units
- A material, disruptive succession-related event affecting capital-
  allocation continuity

## Competitive advantages — detail

*(YAML holds concise claims only, per the frozen schema; fuller evidence
and counterarguments live here.)*

1. **Negative-cost insurance float at scale.** Evidence: ~$176.9B of float,
   underwriting profits (not losses) in both Q1 2026 and Q1 2025, producing
   a negative average cost of float in both periods. [BRK-C02, BRK-C03]
   Counterargument: underwriting profitability is not guaranteed to persist
   -- a single severe catastrophe year or a hardening-then-softening
   pricing cycle could turn the cost of float positive, and the evidence
   bundle covers only two specific quarters, not a multi-year underwriting
   track record.
2. **Exceptional balance-sheet liquidity and capital mobility.** Evidence:
   $236.8B of cash/Treasury-bills, ~$285.0B of equity securities, and up to
   ~$33B of insurance-subsidiary ordinary-dividend capacity without prior
   regulatory approval. [BRK-C04, BRK-C05, BRK-C08] Counterargument: this
   liquidity is a capacity to deploy capital, not evidence of what it will
   be deployed into or at what prospective return -- the evidence bundle
   does not itself evaluate specific capital-allocation decisions' quality.
3. **Decentralized, diversified operating-business base.** Evidence:
   insurance/reinsurance, railroad, utilities/energy, manufacturing,
   service, and retail businesses under one capital-allocation umbrella.
   [BRK-C01] Counterargument: the evidence bundle does not provide
   segment-level operating-earnings detail for the non-insurance segments,
   so this record cannot independently verify each segment's own
   competitive position or cyclicality from the sources inspected.

## Capital priority and next-dollar considerations

**Business quality**, per the evidence above, shows an exceptionally
liquid, structurally advantaged insurance-and-conglomerate platform with a
negative-cost float and very large capital-deployment capacity, offset by
real, disclosed reserve-estimation, goodwill-concentration, and
succession-execution risk, and by an evidence base that documents the
insurance segment far more fully than the non-insurance operating
businesses.

**Capital priority is a separate question, and BRK.B's own current T2
placement (1.65% target) is itself a data point this record preserves as
historical policy, not evidence.** Whether the next investment dollar is
better spent on BRK.B than on another T2 name or another governed holding
entirely is not settled by this record: this record does not perform a
cross-holding capital-priority ranking, and no numerical score or
composite index is produced. **What can be said qualitatively:** BRK.B's
float/liquidity evidence is unusually strong relative to this record's own
evidence base for other holdings, but the absence of segment-level detail
for the non-insurance businesses is a real, disclosed limitation on how
completely this record can characterize the whole enterprise. **What would
be lost if BRK.B were absent:** the portfolio's only direct exposure to a
large-scale, negative-cost-float insurance-and-diversified-industrial
capital-allocation model -- a distinct economic structure from every other
T2 holding in this batch.

**Factors that could support greater capital priority** include BRK.B's
negative cost of float, its exceptional liquidity, and its disclosed
capital-mobility capacity. **Factors favoring an alternative use of
capital** include the disclosed reserve and goodwill-concentration risks,
the succession-execution uncertainty, and the evidence base's limited
segment-level detail outside insurance. A reviewer weighting the float/
liquidity strength more heavily could reasonably favor greater priority; a
reviewer weighting the succession and segment-evidence gaps more heavily
could reasonably favor less. **This record does not resolve that question
and does not rank BRK.B against any other holding.**

Business quality and portfolio capital priority are separate judgments.
This record does not determine the governed target: BRK.B's current T2
tier and 1.65% target remain binding, this Intelligence record does not
change them, and a final next-dollar decision requires portfolio-wide
comparison and principal approval.
