## Business summary

Applied Materials is the largest US-based semiconductor equipment maker by
revenue, supplying equipment across the wafer fabrication process:
deposition (CVD/PVD/ALD), etch, ion implantation, chemical-mechanical
planarization (CMP), and metrology/inspection — the broadest per-tool
process-step coverage of any company in this four-company batch. Three
segments: **Semiconductor Systems** (equipment sales, largest and most
cyclical), **Applied Global Services / AGS** (recurring service contracts,
spares, upgrades — has grown faster than Systems in recent quarters), and a
smaller **Display and Adjacent Markets** segment (panel-maker capex
dependent).

FY2025 (fiscal year ended 2025-10-26): record revenue $28.37B (+4% YoY, the
company's sixth consecutive year of growth), net income $6.998B (-2% YoY),
GAAP diluted EPS $8.66 (record, +1% YoY); non-GAAP diluted EPS $9.42 (+9%
YoY). China represented an estimated 28-30% of FY2025 revenue depending on
basis (see unresolved-conflicts note above). Q2 FY2026 (reported
2026-05-14): record revenue $7.91B (+11% YoY); Semiconductor Systems
$5.97-6.0B (+10-14% YoY); AGS $1.67B (+17% YoY); the company raised its
calendar-2026 semiconductor-equipment growth outlook to >30% at this
report. Q1 FY2026 (reported 2026-02-12): revenue $7.0B (-2% YoY); China
revenue specifically $2.1B (30% of total, down 6.6% YoY) — the sequence
Q1-to-Q2 FY2026 (30% -> 24% of revenue from China) illustrates the
lumpy/uneven nature of AMAT's China exposure under active export-control
pressure, discussed further below.

## Investment thesis (business-quality description, not a recommendation)

**Concise statement:** Applied Materials is the broadest-coverage
semiconductor-equipment supplier in this batch, with a record FY2025 and an
accelerating Q2 FY2026 — but it carries material, actively-moving
China-revenue exposure (though not the batch's highest single-period peak
— see the batch comparison artifact), a confirmed historical
export-compliance violation (a February 2026 BIS settlement), and repeated
regulatory pressure.

**Expanded narrative:** AMAT's breadth (deposition, etch, CMP, ion
implantation, metrology all under one roof, plus a large recurring-service
business) is its most distinctive structural feature relative to the other
three companies in this batch, each of which is comparatively more
specialized (ASML in lithography, KLAC in process control/inspection, LRCX
in etch/deposition with heavier memory concentration). That breadth has
produced record results (FY2025 revenue, Q2 FY2026 revenue) and expanding
margins even while the company absorbs real, disclosed, and repeated
China-export-control costs — three separate dated tightening waves in
roughly three years (October 2022, October 2023, September 2025), each
with its own disclosed dollar impact, plus a reported (unofficial,
unresolved) Chinese domestic-content mandate that would independently cap
AMAT's addressable China share regardless of any further US action.
Separately, and distinct from the general industry-wide export-control
environment, AMAT itself has a confirmed compliance-violation history: a
February 2026 BIS settlement ($252.5 million civil penalty, the
second-highest on record) for illegal reexports of equipment to SMIC
between 2021-2022 — direct evidence about AMAT's own past export-control
conduct, not merely industry-wide regulatory risk. The October 2025
workforce reduction (~4%, ~1,400 jobs) is a concrete, recent data point
this record does not minimize, though its cause is genuinely mixed across
sources: the company's own stated rationale emphasized automation and
restructuring, while the announcement's timing coincided with the
September 2025 BIS rule's disclosed revenue impact — this record states
both facts without asserting a single cause.

**Conditions relevant to monitoring this thesis** (qualitative, observable):
- Q3 FY2026 results against the company's own guidance (~$8.95B revenue,
  ~$3.36 non-GAAP EPS)
- China revenue share stabilizing rather than continuing to swing
  unpredictably quarter to quarter
- No further material regulatory escalation beyond the September 2025 BIS
  Affiliates Rule
- No confirmed instance of a Chinese domestic competitor (Naura, AMEC)
  qualifying on a leading-edge line at a major non-Chinese foundry (the
  AMEC/TSMC claim above remains unverified, not confirmed)

**Conditions that would weaken confidence in the above:**
- A confirmed, primary-sourced instance of a Chinese domestic-equipment
  maker qualifying on an advanced (sub-14nm-class) line at a major
  non-Chinese customer
- Formal confirmation and quantification of China's domestic-content
  mandate at the regulatory-text level, with a disclosed AMAT revenue
  impact
- A further workforce reduction or a formal guidance cut tied to
  export-control impact beyond what is already disclosed
- A material, sustained deceleration inconsistent with the >30% CY2026
  growth outlook the company itself raised in May 2026

**Export-control shock timeline (dated instances) — kept separate from
AMAT's own compliance-violation history below:**
- **October 2022** — BIS export controls on semiconductor manufacturing
  equipment/technology to China. AMAT revised Q4 FY2022 guidance down (from
  $6.65B +/- $400M to $6.4B +/- $250M), attributing an estimated ~$400M
  +/- $150M negative impact to the new rules.
- **October 2023** — a further BIS rule update; AMAT-specific quantified
  dollar impact for this specific update was not found in this research
  pass — flagged as a gap, not asserted as zero impact.
- **September 29, 2025 ("BIS Affiliates Rule")** — expanded Entity List
  coverage to subsidiaries of already-blacklisted Chinese companies. AMAT
  disclosed an estimated ~$110M Q4 FY2025 and ~$600M FY2026 revenue impact
  — a separately reported $710M figure is simply the sum of these two
  disclosed figures, not a separate or conflicting number (corrected in
  this remediation pass).
- **October 23, 2025** — AMAT announced a ~4% (~1,400 jobs) workforce
  reduction with a $160-180M associated charge, mostly recognized in Q4
  FY2025. The company's own stated rationale (per secondary paraphrase)
  emphasized automation/digitalization/geographic-shift-driven
  restructuring; multiple outlets separately noted the announcement's
  temporal proximity to the BIS Affiliates Rule above, but no source found
  in this research establishes that export controls directly caused the
  reduction — this record states both facts without asserting a single
  cause (corrected in this remediation pass from an earlier draft that
  asserted direct causation).

**AMAT's own confirmed export-compliance violation (distinct from the
shock timeline above — this is about AMAT's own past conduct, not the
regulatory environment):** on 2026-02-11, Applied Materials, Inc. and its
subsidiary Applied Materials Korea, Ltd. entered into a settlement
agreement with BIS, paying a $252.5 million civil penalty — the statutory
maximum, and the second-highest BIS penalty on record (after Seagate
Technology's $300M in 2023) — for 56 violations of the Export
Administration Regulations: unauthorized reexport and attempted reexport
of semiconductor manufacturing equipment (approximately $126M in
ion-implantation equipment, assembled in South Korea, reportedly using
largely US-origin parts) to Semiconductor Manufacturing International
Corporation (SMIC) and its subsidiaries — a BIS Entity List party since
December 2020 — during approximately March 2021 to June 2022. The
settlement also requires two internal export-compliance audits and imposes
a three-year suspended denial order. This was a fact missed in the
original research pass, added in this remediation pass after an external
primary-source cross-check flagged it; it is cross-corroborated across
multiple independent legal/trade-press outlets and matches BIS's own
press-release title, though this session's own WebFetch attempt against
bis.gov returned HTTP 403 and the settlement documents themselves were not
directly opened by this session.

**Chinese domestic-competitor progression** (secondary, consistent across
multiple sources but not primary-verified): Naura's nine-month FY2025
revenue (27.14B yuan) was roughly 4-5x its full-year-2020 level; AMEC's
revenue was reported "more than 5x" its 2020 level over the same
comparison window. Naura has deployed etch tools at 14nm and is reportedly
testing 7nm; its oxidation/diffusion furnaces reportedly account for over
60% of tooling on SMIC's 28nm lines. China's domestic-substitution rate for
etch and thin-film deposition reportedly exceeded 40% in one source,
concentrated in mature-node (28nm/14nm) applications.

## Margin-relevance evidence (factual, advisory only — no leverage recommendation)

Per `PI-0023` SSB.16 and `OPS-0006` SS4's Milestone 3 requirement.

- **Cyclicality**: the 2019 memory-driven downturn is the clearest
  historically documented episode — FY2019 revenue guided down
  approximately 16% amid a DRAM industry revenue decline of roughly 37.5%
  (broader memory market -31.5%), itself caused by a hyperscaler demand
  pullback exposing excess OEM inventory beginning late 2018. AMAT's stock
  reportedly jumped ~86% during calendar 2019 itself — i.e., equity
  recovery led fundamental recovery in that specific cycle, a pattern
  worth noting but not further quantified in this research pass. A 2022
  downturn (Oct 2022 export-control shock plus broader macro/rate-hike
  tightening) saw AMAT stock decline an estimated 30-42% depending on
  source (not reconciled to a single figure); a separate 2018 episode saw
  the stock down an estimated ~40% amid broadly weak semiconductor demand,
  driver not further detailed in sources found. One third-party analytical
  source (Trefis, not independently verified) characterizes AMAT as having
  weathered 15 major historical shocks with an average peak-to-trough stock
  decline of 23% (vs. 16% for the S&P 500 over the same episodes) and a
  single deepest historical drawdown of approximately 55%.
- **Balance-sheet strength**: total cash + investments approximately
  $12.9B at FY2025 year-end, approximately $13.48B at end of Q1 FY2026;
  total debt approximately $6.56B at FY2025 year-end — cash/investments
  covering total debt roughly 2x per these secondary figures. Debt
  maturity schedule, interest rates, and credit ratings were not confirmed
  in this research pass — flagged as a gap.
- **Semiconductor-capex sensitivity**: direct and structural, shared
  demand driver across the batch; AMAT's exposure spans foundry/logic
  (Q2 FY2026 Semiconductor Systems end-market mix reported as
  foundry/logic-and-other 67%, DRAM 29%, NAND/Flash 4% — secondary,
  unverified against a primary transcript), so AMAT is sensitive to both
  logic-capex and memory-pricing cycles rather than concentrated in either.
- **Export-control/geopolitical exposure**: frequently-shocked on a
  *dollar-impact disclosure* basis (three separate dated, quantified
  regulatory impacts in roughly three years, plus a reported unofficial
  domestic-content mandate) — this record does not claim AMAT is the most
  exposed of the four companies in this batch on this dimension, only that
  it is well-documented and material; see the Risks section above and the
  batch comparison artifact for the cross-company comparison. AMAT is also
  the only company in this batch with a confirmed historical
  export-compliance violation of its own (the February 2026 BIS
  settlement) — a distinct, company-specific data point from the shared
  regulatory-environment exposure the other companies in this batch also
  face.
- **Customer concentration**: FY2025 10-K reportedly discloses two
  customers at approximately 19% and approximately 15% of net revenue
  (~34% combined) — secondary-sourced, customer identities not disclosed
  in any source found, not independently verified against the primary
  filing.
- **Recovery uncertainty / thesis-deterioration detectability**: AMAT's
  China exposure moves unevenly and with a lag relative to the underlying
  regulatory action (shipment deferrals/cancellations happen around license
  timing, not announcement timing), meaning a given quarter's China-revenue
  print may not immediately reveal the full magnitude of a new
  restriction — a detectability characteristic worth noting for any future
  margin-relevant monitoring, stated descriptively only.

## What exposure would be lost if AMAT were absent from the portfolio

AMAT is the only company in this batch with meaningful direct exposure
across the full breadth of deposition, etch, ion implantation, CMP, and
metrology in a single company, plus the batch's largest recurring-service
(AGS) revenue base. Removing AMAT would concentrate the portfolio's
semiconductor-capital-equipment exposure more heavily into LRCX's
etch/deposition (with materially higher memory-cycle concentration per
LRCX's own record), KLAC's process-control specialization, and ASML's
lithography exclusivity — none of which replicates AMAT's combined
process-step breadth or its scale of recurring-service revenue.
