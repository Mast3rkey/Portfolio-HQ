## Business summary

WM is North America's largest comprehensive environmental/waste-services
provider, serving 20M+ residential, commercial, industrial, and municipal
customers. [WM-C01] Physical footprint (secondary aggregation, not
independently verified): ~262 landfill sites (the largest network in
US/Canada), 339 transfer stations, 105 recycling facilities, 413
collection operations. Core lines of business: Collection, Landfill,
Transfer, Recycling, and a growing "Other" category encompassing
Renewable Energy and Healthcare Solutions (from the 2024-closed
Stericycle acquisition, medical/regulated waste services).

FY2025 revenue was **$25.2 billion**, up from $22.1 billion in FY2024 --
the increase largely reflects the first full year of Stericycle
consolidation. A line-of-business revenue cut (secondary aggregator,
Bullfincher, not WM's formal reportable-segment structure) shows:
Commercial $6.52B (26.05%), Landfill $5.35B (21.36%), Industrial $3.99B
(15.93%), Residential $3.60B (14.37%), Healthcare Solutions $2.95B
(11.79%), WM Renewable Energy $318M (1.44%) -- this cut is directionally
indicative only, not confirmed against the 10-K directly. **Disclosed
explicitly, per independent review (2026-08-05):** these six line items
sum to only ~$22.73B, roughly 90% of the stated $25.2B FY2025 total --
a ~$2.5B gap this record's original draft did not flag. A targeted
WebSearch re-check this session found additional figures from the same
aggregator source for two lines not in the original six -- Transfer
~$2.63B and Recycling Processing and Sales ~$1.26B for FY2025 -- but
adding these to the original six *overshoots* the stated $25.2B total
by roughly $1.4B rather than closing the gap, consistent with these
being two different, non-additive classification views (a
customer-facing Commercial/Industrial/Residential/Landfill/Healthcare/
Renewable line-of-business cut versus a service-type Collection/
Landfill/Transfer/Recycling/Other cut that likely embeds Transfer and
Recycling revenue inside the customer-facing lines already) rather than
components of one single complete breakdown. This session could not
reconcile WM's FY2025 revenue to a single, complete, non-overlapping
segment/line-of-business breakdown from the secondary sources
available -- treat every revenue-mix figure in this record as
partial and directionally indicative only, not a complete accounting.

## The Stericycle acquisition

Closed **2024-11-04**: all outstanding Stericycle shares acquired for
**$62.00/share cash**, total enterprise value ~$7.2B (including debt
assumption/repayment figures cited somewhat inconsistently across
sources -- ~$0.5B assumed + ~$0.8B repaid in one characterization vs.
~$1.4B net debt in another), a 24% premium to Stericycle's 60-day
volume-weighted average price. Funded via WM's Term Credit Agreement and
commercial paper, subsequently repaid via a $5.2B senior notes issuance
priced October 2024. [WM-R01]

**Synergy target -- unreconciled escalation**: originally "run-rate cost
synergies exceeding $125 million" at announcement; as of the Q2 2026
call, management cites progress toward **">$300 million of total
synergies by end of 2027"** -- a materially larger, later-stated target
whose origin and timing of increase this session could not trace. Flagged
explicitly as an unreconciled figure, not confirmed guidance history.

**Integration history -- documented friction through 2025, characterized
as resolved by mid-2026**: multiple Waste Dive articles across Q2-Q4 2025
disclosed customer onboarding and billing/ERP integration issues --
credits and deferred price increases were offered to affected customers,
and Q3 2025 Healthcare Solutions revenue ($628M) came in slightly below
expectations, attributed partly to ERP integration friction. Separately,
Stericycle (pre-acquisition, as an independent company) paid a $2M
settlement in a New York overbilling complaint -- this session could not
confirm whether the underlying conduct predates or postdates the WM
acquisition. By Q4 2025, management described the segment as "turning a
corner." **At the Q2 2026 call (the specific quarter this record's gate
names)**, CEO Jim Fish characterized Healthcare Solutions as fully
integrated: *"We feel like we've fixed the business now and integrated
it, this is really turning into exactly what we hoped when we bought it
initially."* Segment adjusted operating EBITDA margin expanded to **19%**
from 17% a year earlier; SG&A as a percentage of revenue improved ~290
basis points; cross-selling has generated $32M of cumulative annualized
EBITDA to date. **This is a self-reported management characterization,
not independently audited by this session.**

## Recent financial results

**Q2 2026** (reported/discussed 2026-07-27 through 2026-07-29): revenue
$6.684B (+4.0% YoY); net income $785M or $786M (a $1M discrepancy across
secondary sources, unreconciled); adjusted EBITDA $2.067B (+5.5% reported,
+9.1% excluding 2025 wildfire-cleanup comparisons); adjusted EBITDA
margin ~30.9%; adjusted EPS $2.02 (beat consensus $1.98); free cash flow
$1.104B for the quarter alone (+34.5-35% YoY); H1 2026 cumulative
operating cash flow $3.23B, H1 free cash flow $2.02B; shareholder
returns >$1B in the quarter (dividends + buybacks).

Segment detail: combined Recycling + Renewable Energy adjusted operating
EBITDA **$163M**, up 32.5% from $123M a year earlier, driven by higher
recycling volumes, automation efficiencies, and increased RNG production.
Full-year collection & disposal volume guidance was revised to a ~1%
decline (or ~50bps ex-wildfire-comp effects), attributed by CEO Fish to
lack of recovery from Q1 winter-weather volume loss and specific lost
national accounts in the commercial segment -- not broad economic
weakness, per management's own characterization.

**Full-year 2026 guidance, revised at Q2**: revenue narrowed down to
$26.275B-$26.475B (from $26.43B-$26.63B post-Q1, ~0.5% cut), attributed
to softer volumes, RNG pipeline-connection delays (two completed plants
awaiting third-party pipeline connection), and softer recycling
brokerage; adjusted EBITDA maintained at $8.15B-$8.25B; adjusted EBITDA
margin guidance raised 20bps to 31.0%-31.2%; free cash flow maintained
at $3.75B-$3.85B. H1 2026 capex $1.28B, down from $1.563B in H1 2025 --
normalizing as the sustainability capital program nears completion.

**Q1 2026** (reported ~2026-04-29): revenue $6.23B; net income $723M;
adjusted EBITDA ~$1.85B (+5.9% YoY, margin 29.8%, +70bps); core price
+6.3%, yield +3.9%; free cash flow $920M; Healthcare Solutions ~12%
operating EBITDA growth.

Next earnings date: Q3 2026, expected 2026-10-27 before market open
(not independently confirmed against WM's own IR calendar, which could

## Sustainability business (RNG and recycling automation)

A $3 billion 2022-2026 growth-capital program targets 39 new/upgraded
recycling facilities and 20 new WM-owned Renewable Natural Gas (landfill-
gas-to-pipeline) facilities, with >$1.4B of the total earmarked for the
RNG buildout specifically. As of April 2025, 8 of 20 RNG facilities and
27 of 39 recycling facilities were complete; the Q1 2026 call disclosed
seven new RNG facilities had come online since Q1 2025. Renewable-energy
EBITDA more than doubled year-over-year at Q1 2026; recycling EBITDA grew
18% despite a 27% decline in commodity prices. A prior WM-stated target
projected the completed RNG network to generate $600M adjusted operating
EBITDA and $450M free cash flow in 2026 -- the Q2 2026 combined
recycling+RNG EBITDA figure ($163M for the quarter) implies a full-year
run-rate roughly consistent with that target if sustained, though this
session did not independently confirm the reconciliation.

## Competitive position

Main competitor: **Republic Services (RSG)** -- operates a landfill
network cited inconsistently as ~190 or ~209 active landfills across
different sources (vs. WM's ~262, giving WM a larger disposal-capacity
network on either reading). RSG FY2025 revenue ~$17.1B (vs. WM's $25.2B
-- WM is meaningfully larger, partly explained by Healthcare Solutions,
which RSG has no equivalent to). A third major public competitor, Waste
Connections (WCN), was not researched this session -- a disclosed gap.
Ongoing private-equity roll-up activity in smaller regional haulers was
also noted (e.g., TPG's agreements to acquire Waste Eliminator and
Liberty Waste Solutions, expected to close Q3 2026) as competitive-
landscape color below WM/RSG scale.

**Moat characteristics**: landfill permitting is described in secondary
investment-analysis commentary as extremely difficult -- in most
jurisdictions practically impossible -- to newly obtain, a process that
can take over a decade and cost hundreds of millions of dollars (one
source) or "$50M+ in capital and years of permitting" (another, less
extreme figure -- inconsistent magnitude across sources, directional
only). Existing permitted landfills are characterized as essentially
irreplaceable assets; the industry is described as having consolidated
into an effective oligopoly given permitting barriers plus route-density
economies of scale in collection, with regional operations tending to
function as de facto regional monopolies.

## Material risks

1. **Stericycle integration** -- documented 2025 friction, management-
   characterized as resolved by Q2 2026 (self-reported, not
   independently audited).
2. **Unreconciled synergy-target escalation** ($125M → ">$300M") --
   origin/timing not traced this session.
3. **Commodity-price exposure in recycling** -- ~$27M EBITDA impact per
   $10/ton move in single-stream commodity prices (secondary
   characterization); historically volatile (60-150% peak-to-trough
   swings); partial, not complete, insulation via volume/automation.
4. **PFAS regulatory/litigation exposure** -- a live, industry-wide issue
   as of 2026 (EPA TSCA reporting-rule revision, April 2026; industry
   litigation over CERCLA hazardous-substance designation; a lobbying
   push for a landfill liability exemption with no confirmed legislative
   movement). One specific North Carolina PFAS groundwater lawsuit was
   found but this session could not confirm WM ownership/operation of
   that landfill. **No WM-specific named PFAS litigation, defendant
   status, or reserve figure was found** -- a disclosed research gap, not
   a finding of no exposure, given WM's landfill-network scale.
5. **FY2026 revenue guidance trim** at Q2 2026 (softer volumes, RNG
   pipeline-connection delays, softer recycling brokerage) -- EBITDA/FCF
   guidance maintained and margin guidance raised, so the softness has
   not yet flowed through to profitability guidance, but worth
   monitoring.
6. **Landfill closure/post-closure liability reserves** -- not
   independently quantified this session; a disclosed gap.
7. **Fuel/labor cost inflation** -- not separately quantified this
   session; a disclosed gap.
8. **Valuation evidence** -- primary source access is now resolved, but the

## Investment thesis (descriptive, no recommendation)

**Concise statement:** WM's collection/landfill/transfer core is a
structurally moated, cash-generative business with an exceptional
capital-return track record; the Stericycle acquisition added real
diversification but carried documented integration friction now
characterized (by management, unverified) as resolved; the gate's
purchase-valuation concern remains genuinely contested across sources
even after this research.

**Conditions that would support initiation**: a reproducible valuation with
an adequate margin across sensitivity cases; continued Healthcare Solutions
integration evidence; resolution of the Stericycle synergy-target
inconsistency; and a WM-specific PFAS-exposure figure, if one exists.

**Conditions that would argue for continued caution**: a further
Healthcare Solutions integration setback contradicting management's
"fixed" characterization; a material PFAS-liability development
specific to WM; sustained volume declines beyond the disclosed Q2 2026
guidance trim.
