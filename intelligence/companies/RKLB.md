## Business summary

Rocket Lab operates two segments: **Launch Services** (Electron,
small-lift, operational since 2018, plus the suborbital HASTE variant;
Neutron, medium-lift, in development, not yet flown) and **Space
Systems** (satellite components/subsystems and full spacecraft
manufacturing). [RKLB-C05] The Space Systems segment was built primarily
through acquisition: Sinclair Interplanetary (satellite hardware,
April 2020), Advanced Solutions Inc. (flight software/guidance, October
2021), Planetary Systems Corporation (spacecraft separation systems,
December 2021), SolAero Holdings (space solar power, ~$80M cash, January
2022), Geost LLC (EO/IR sensors -- missile warning/tracking, ISR, space
domain awareness -- announced 2025-05-27, closed 2025-08-12, ~$275M),
Mynaric AG (laser optical communications terminals, RKLB's first
European footprint, closed 2026-04-14, $155.3M aggregate consideration),
and Motiv Space Systems (space robotics -- built the Perseverance rover's
robotic arm -- signed 2026-05-07, closed 2026-05-26, terms undisclosed,
rebranding to "Rocket Lab Robotics").

Q1 2026 segment revenue: Space Systems $136.7M (+57.2% YoY); Launch
Services $63.7M (+78.9% YoY) -- summing to the reported $200.3M total
within rounding. The largest disclosed Space Systems customer by backlog
is the U.S. Space Development Agency, with over $1.3B in satellite prime
contracts, including an 18-space-vehicle Tranche 2 Transport Layer Beta
contract with deployments slated mid-2027.

## Neutron program -- status, and the central open question for the gate

**Timeline**: originally targeted earlier, delayed to "no earlier than
late 2026" as of 2025-11-11. On the 2026-05-07 Q1 earnings call, CEO
Peter Beck reaffirmed **Q4 2026** as the first-flight target, and
subsequent mid-2026 updates continue to describe Neutron as "on track for
first flight by year-end 2026."

**A setback occurred 2026-01-21**: a Neutron Stage 1 tank ruptured during
a hydrostatic pressure trial at RKLB's Space Structures Complex in
Middle River, Maryland -- attributed by Beck to a manufacturing defect in
a tank hand-laid by a third-party contractor while RKLB's own automated
fiber-placement machine was still being commissioned. This was described
in one source as the second Neutron schedule slip in just over a year.

**Progress since**: by the May 2026 call, Beck described "tremendous
strides" on Stage 1 tank design refinements (improved strength margins
and manufacturability), and specifically told investors to watch for
"placing of items on test stands" as the near-term progress indicator.
Disclosed hardware status: first-stage structure qualification underway
at Middle River, a first stage on a test stand undergoing launch/
re-entry/landing load testing, and the fairing system at Wallops being
prepped for integrated assembly. Launch Complex 3 (Wallops Island, Pad
0D) opened 2025-08-28 and is described as operational. RKLB has filed
launch permits with the FAA for a debut window of 2026-07-01 through
2026-12-31 -- described as the company's first official federal-
regulatory timeline submission specific to Neutron.

**Commercial demand**: RKLB stated it sold more Neutron launches in Q1
2026 alone than in all of FY2025, around a "five-launch Neutron deal"
announced with the May 2026 earnings release.

**Assessment for gate purposes (fact only, no opinion offered)**: no
first flight had occurred as of 2026-08-05. **This record could not
identify a single, sourced, unambiguous RKLB-stated milestone matching
this gate's own next_gate language ("a defined Neutron technical
milestone")** -- different secondary sources describe stage/component-
level test progress inconsistently, and Beck's own most concrete public
framing ("placing of items on test stands") is qualitative, not a single
named, dated technical gate. **This should be treated as an open
verification item requiring a future session's direct review of RKLB's
own investor materials, not a resolved question.**

## The Iridium acquisition -- by far the largest current integration question

**Deal existence and headline terms -- sourcing note added 2026-08-05
following independent review**: this record's original draft treated the
deal's existence as settled fact from general news-outlet coverage
alone. A targeted re-check this session found the deal independently
corroborated via both parties' own official investor-relations channels
identified by search -- `investor.iridium.com` and
`investors.rocketlabcorp.com`/`rocketlabcorp.com` press releases dated
2026-06-29, plus a specific SEC EDGAR exhibit URL under Rocket Lab's own
CIK (1819994) -- a meaningfully stronger corroboration pattern than
general trade-press coverage alone, though **none of these pages was
directly opened this session** (WebFetch remained blocked on all
attempted domains, including `sec.gov` and `iridium.com`, on a fresh
attempt). Treat the deal's existence and headline terms ($54.00/share,
~$8.0B enterprise value) as multi-outlet-corroborated, including by both
companies' own official channels, but **not primary-source-confirmed**
by this session's own direct reading.

Announced **2026-06-29**, likely after (or concurrent with) this gate's
original drafting and materially larger than any prior RKLB acquisition
combined: [RKLB-R01]

- **Structure**: cash-and-stock; Iridium shareholders receive $27.00 cash
  plus RKLB stock (within a price collar) for combined value of
  $54.00/Iridium share -- a 24.1% premium to Iridium's last close.
- **Financing**: RKLB secured commitments for a **$3.6 billion bridge
  loan** from Deutsche Bank and Wells Fargo to fund the cash portion,
  plus cash on hand and "additional debt and equity financing."
- **Expected close**: mid-2027, subject to Iridium stockholder approval,
  antitrust/communications-regulatory clearance (Iridium is a licensed
  spectrum operator, triggering real regulatory review distinct from a
  typical bolt-on acquisition), Form S-4 effectiveness, and other
  customary conditions.
- **Strategic rationale** (company/press framing): combines RKLB's
  launch and spacecraft-manufacturing capability with Iridium's global
  L-band satellite network, licensed spectrum, and 2.5M+ subscribers
  (government, defense, aviation, maritime, commercial), positioned in
  coverage as competing directly with SpaceX's Starlink in communications
  services.
- **Important qualifier**: Iridium's existing constellation is expected
  to support operations to ~2035, with no full replacement planned before
  2030 -- meaning this deal does **not** hand Neutron an immediate large
  replacement-launch manifest, contrary to what might otherwise be
  assumed.
- **Market reaction**: initial pop (RKLB +16%, Iridium +25%) and several
  analyst price-target increases; subsequent commentary describes RKLB
  stock "surrendering roughly a third of its value in a single month"
  after the initial pop, attributed in muddled secondary commentary to a
  mix of financing/dilution concerns, CEO insider selling (see Risks),
  the Neutron delay news, and broader sector rotation -- this record does
  not adopt any single causal attribution from that commentary.

Smaller, already-closed acquisitions (Geost, Mynaric, Motiv Space
Systems -- see Business summary) are likely what this gate's original
"acquisition integration" language referred to; **Iridium is a
substantially larger and more complex integration question that any
future gate reassessment should weigh explicitly, not treat as a
footnote.**

## Competitive position

Launch competitors: SpaceX (dominant, Falcon 9), Firefly Aerospace
(public since August 2025, stock roughly halved after a rocket-stage
test explosion), Relativity Space (developing Terran R, targeting
late-2026 first launch), Stoke Space (pursuing full reusability, also an
NSSL Phase 3 Lane 1 provider alongside RKLB). RKLB and Stoke Space were
selected for **NSSL Phase 3 Lane 1** (March 2025); the Space Force raised
the Lane 1 ceiling from $5.6B to $17B, covering ~170 national-security
missions through FY2029 -- but program eligibility does not guarantee
individual wins: on 2026-07-29, the Space Force awarded SpaceX (not
RKLB) all 18 task-order missions in a related Space-Based Sensing and
Targeting competition worth $1.6B.

RKLB and Raytheon were selected (2026-05-07, one of 12 companies/teams
named) for the Space-Based Interceptor/Golden Dome missile-defense
demonstration program (initial capability demonstration targeted 2028).
RKLB separately won a **$266 million Space Force contract** (announced
2026-07-27) for up to 18 suborbital launches supporting missile-defense/
hypersonic testing -- described by the company as its largest launch
contract to date.

**Electron track record**: total mission count was cited inconsistently
across sources (84 missions as of ~March 2026 per one source; 91
missions/95.6% success rate per another, undated, source)
`[UNVERIFIED-CONFLICT]` -- the true current total as of 2026-08-05 is
likely in the high-80s to low-90s but could not be pinned to an exact
number this session. Three confirmed launch anomalies in company history
(2020-07-04 second-stage power failure; 2021-05-15 second-stage

not adopted as fact either way.

## Material risks

1. **Neutron execution/schedule risk** -- no first flight as of this
   record's date; two disclosed schedule slips; no single confirmed
   "defined technical milestone" identified matching the gate's own
   language.
2. **Iridium acquisition integration** -- a transformative, ~$8B deal
   substantially larger than any prior RKLB acquisition, not closing
   until mid-2027, requiring shareholder and antitrust/spectrum-
   regulatory approval.
3. **Continuing GAAP losses and active dilution** -- net loss every year
   since inception through FY2025; two large 2026 ATM programs; share
   count rising through the year without a single confirmed current
   figure.
4. **Customer concentration** -- ~47% of FY2025 revenue government-
   related; top 5 customers 49% of revenue; top 5 backlog customers 77%
   of backlog (secondary-sourced, not independently confirmed).
5. **Launch-failure risk** (operational, inherent to the business) --
   three confirmed Electron anomalies historically; Neutron is an
   unflown vehicle with unquantifiable first-flight failure risk, and
   secondary commentary frames Neutron as having "evolved from a
   technical milestone into the primary financial catalyst" for the
   investment case, raising the stakes of any first-flight setback.
6. **Competitive/pricing pressure** -- SpaceX's continued dominance,
   including winning task orders within programs RKLB itself
   participates in.
7. **Governance/sentiment fact** -- CEO Peter Beck's large stock sale
   (~$286.4M, July 2026, under a pre-existing 10b5-1 plan) drew explicit
   media scrutiny concurrent with the Iridium announcement.
8. **This session's own weak source access** -- no primary document
   directly opened; several figures internally inconsistent across
   sources (see disclosure above).

## Investment thesis (descriptive, no recommendation)

**Concise statement:** Rocket Lab shows a genuinely strong, reliability-
proven Electron launch business and a rapidly growing, diversifying
Space Systems segment, but the investment case increasingly depends on
two large, currently unresolved execution questions -- Neutron's
first flight and the Iridium acquisition's integration -- that did not
both exist in their current form when this gate was likely first
written.

**Conditions that would support initiation**: a successful, on-schedule
(or only modestly delayed) Neutron first flight; a clearly stated,
RKLB-confirmed technical milestone satisfying the gate's own language;
continued backlog/liquidity strength through Q2 2026 and beyond; early,
credible signs of Iridium-deal regulatory progress without a material
adverse condition.

**Conditions that would argue for continued caution**: a further Neutron
schedule slip or a first-flight failure; Iridium-deal delay, material
condition, or termination; accelerating dilution beyond current ATM
programs; a further large insider sale inconsistent with confidence in
near-term execution.
