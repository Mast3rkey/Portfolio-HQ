# RKLB — Rocket Lab Corporation

Last updated: 2026-09-07. This refresh directly reads Rocket Lab's SEC-filed
Q2 2026 results and Form 10-Q. It replaces the prior search-snippet evidence
posture, resolves the major financial-data conflicts, and defines an observable
Neutron checkpoint for the next gate review. It does not change the gate,
target, holding, margin policy, or any execution state.

## Decision summary

Rocket Lab reported record Q2 revenue and backlog, but the mix matters: Space
Systems drove the growth while Launch Services revenue declined 4% despite one
additional mission. The company remains GAAP-loss-making and cash-consuming,
and its liquidity was built primarily through substantial equity issuance.

The evidence supports continued monitoring, not gate clearance. Q2 results now
satisfy the financial-information half of the existing gate. They do not
satisfy its technical half. Management described unspecified Neutron progress
as "critical milestones," while also saying the end-of-year launch window was
narrowing and that timing depended on first-stage assembly and test outcomes.
That language is not an independently testable milestone.

For the next review, the minimum prospective technical checkpoint is:

> Rocket Lab publicly confirms that the first-flight Stage 1 tank
> completed qualification and that the integrated first stage was delivered to
> Launch Complex 3, with any material test exceptions disclosed.

This checkpoint is defined before the claimed achievement and ties directly to
unfinished first-stage testing and schedule risk. Meeting it would reopen review; it would not
automatically authorize a purchase or gate removal.

## Verified reported facts

### Q2 2026 operations

- Revenue was $234.1 million versus $144.5 million a year earlier, up 62%.
  Space Systems revenue was $189.5 million, up 94%, while Launch Services
  revenue was $44.6 million, down 4%. [RKLB-P01] [RKLB-P02]
- Rocket Lab completed six Electron missions versus five a year earlier.
  Launch revenue still declined because two Q2 missions were HASTE missions for
  which revenue was recognized over time and partly in earlier quarters.
  [RKLB-P02]
- GAAP gross profit was $84.6 million, a derived 36.1% margin. Management's
  non-GAAP gross margin was 41.5%. GAAP operating loss was $57.5 million, net
  loss was $49.3 million, and adjusted EBITDA loss was $8.8 million.
  [RKLB-P01]
- Backlog was $2.356 billion at June 30, with approximately 45% expected to be
  recognized within 12 months. Backlog is contracted work, not revenue or
  profit, and option values should not be treated as firm orders. [RKLB-P02]

### Liquidity, dilution, and acquisition exposure

- Cash and current plus non-current marketable securities totaled $2.388
  billion at June 30. Convertible notes and long-term borrowings totaled $14.8
  million, producing derived net cash of $2.373 billion before leases and
  restricted cash. [RKLB-P01]
- First-half operating cash outflow was $134.4 million. The company raised
  $1.530 billion of gross ATM equity proceeds in the same six-month period,
  before $16.7 million of issuance costs. Liquidity therefore cannot be read as
  evidence of self-funded operations. [RKLB-P01]
- Common shares outstanding rose to 598.2 million at June 30 from 543.6 million
  at December 31, 2025. A further 41.0 million Series A participating preferred
  shares were outstanding, and Q3 guidance uses 641 million basic weighted-
  average shares including those preferred shares. [RKLB-P01]
- Rocket Lab completed the Mynaric and Motiv acquisitions during Q2. Mynaric
  contributed $13.2 million of revenue and a $13.2 million operating loss in the
  quarter; Motiv contributed $1.4 million of revenue and a $0.2 million
  operating loss. These short windows do not establish integration success.
  [RKLB-P02]
- The pending Iridium transaction requires $27.00 cash plus Rocket Lab shares
  for each Iridium share, subject to the merger agreement's collar. It remains
  subject to shareholder, antitrust, FCC, foreign-investment, satellite, and
  telecommunications approvals and is expected to close in 2027. Rocket Lab
  obtained a commitment for a $3.6 billion, 364-day senior secured bridge
  facility. [RKLB-P02]

### Neutron gate evidence

Rocket Lab reported ongoing qualification testing, integration of first-flight
hardware, Archimedes engine testing, and progress on the second stage and
reusable fairing. It said production of the Stage 1 tank was aligned with a
targeted Q4 2026 launch-pad delivery. It also stated that the end-of-year launch
window was narrowing and that exact timing depended on first-stage assembly and
test outcomes. [RKLB-P01] [RKLB-P02]

These are issuer progress claims and schedules. The filing does not report that
the first-flight Stage 1 tank completed qualification, that the integrated first
stage completed acceptance testing, or that the vehicle reached the pad.
Accordingly, the technical gate remains unmet.

## Management outlook

For Q3 2026, management guided to revenue of $250-$265 million, GAAP gross
margin of 29%-31%, non-GAAP gross margin of 35%-37%, GAAP operating expenses of
$143-$149 million, adjusted EBITDA loss of $17-$23 million, and 641 million
basic weighted-average shares including participating preferred shares.
Management did not provide reconciliations for forward-looking non-GAAP gross
margin, operating expenses, or adjusted EBITDA because it said the adjustments
could not be predicted without unreasonable effort. [RKLB-P01]

## Material risks and open items

1. **Neutron remains pre-flight.** The first-flight Stage 1 tank has not
   been reported as qualified or delivered to the pad, and exact launch timing
   depends on unfinished assembly and test outcomes.
2. **Growth is concentrated in Space Systems.** Q2 Launch Services revenue fell
   despite higher mission count; revenue-recognition timing explains part of
   the result but does not erase the mix risk.
3. **Operations consume cash.** First-half operating cash outflow was $134.4
   million, and Q3 guidance calls for another adjusted EBITDA loss.
4. **Dilution is material.** First-half ATM proceeds were $1.530 billion and
   common shares outstanding increased about 10% in six months.
5. **Iridium changes the risk profile.** The pending deal adds large financing,
   regulatory, integration, and stock-consideration risks that the standalone
   valuation screen does not capture.
6. **Backlog quality requires care.** Backlog timing is long-dated, government
   work can be modified or cancelled, and company announcements sometimes
   present totals that include unexercised options.
7. **Acquisition economics are unproven.** Mynaric's Q2 operating loss
   approximately equaled its revenue, while multiple acquisitions expand
   goodwill, intangibles, and execution demands.

## Investment thesis (descriptive, not a recommendation)

Rocket Lab combines a proven small-launch operation with a fast-growing Space
Systems business and a potentially important medium-lift platform. Q2 confirms
commercial momentum and a large contracted backlog. The investment case still
requires Neutron technical proof, durable launch economics, less dependence on
external equity, and credible execution of an Iridium transaction that is much
larger than Rocket Lab's prior acquisitions.

Evidence that could change the disposition:

- successful completion of the prospectively defined Neutron checkpoint;
- transparent disclosure of first-stage test outcomes and launch readiness;
- sustained Space Systems growth with improving acquired-business margins;
- evidence that operating cash consumption is narrowing without continued
  large ATM issuance; and
- final Iridium financing terms plus regulatory and shareholder approvals.

## Conviction

but also material dilution, continuing losses, acquisition complexity, and an
unfinished technical gate. The rating excludes entry price, target weight,
trading, and leverage instructions.
