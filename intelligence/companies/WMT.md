## Business summary

Walmart generated $713.2 billion of FY2026 revenue, including $706.4
billion of net sales. [WMT-C01] It serves approximately 280 million
customers weekly through more than 10,900 stores in 19 countries and its
digital properties, [WMT-C02] operating through three reportable segments:
Walmart U.S., Walmart International, and Sam's Club U.S. [WMT-C03] The
company's EDLP (everyday low price) and EDLC (everyday low cost) model
links customer price leadership to continuous cost discipline. [WMT-C04]
Walmart is expanding higher-margin ecosystem businesses including digital
advertising, marketplace and fulfillment, health and wellness, and
financial services. [WMT-C05]

**Q1 FY2027 results show growth continuing across all three reported
segments, with eCommerce a material contributor.** Walmart U.S. net sales
increased 4.5%, comparable sales increased 4.3%, and eCommerce contributed
approximately 5.2 percentage points to that comparable-sales growth --
meaning eCommerce accounted for the majority of the segment's
comparable-sales gain. [WMT-C06] Walmart International net sales were
$35.1 billion, up 18%, including $2.0 billion of eCommerce growth and $2.3
billion of favorable currency impact -- a meaningful share of the headline
growth figure is currency-driven, not purely organic. [WMT-C07] Sam's Club
U.S. net sales rose 6.1%, with eCommerce contributing approximately 3.1
percentage points to comparable sales. [WMT-C08]

**The evidence bundle's own explicit uncertainty judgment, carried forward
unresolved:** Walmart's scale advantage coexists with risks from thin
retail margins, wage and healthcare inflation, capital intensity, global
sourcing, regulation, litigation, and intense physical and digital
competition. [WMT-C09] This record treats that as an open, unresolved
condition, not a settled durable state.

## Investment thesis

**Concise statement:** Walmart is a dominant-scale, defensive consumer
retailer showing continued comparable-sales growth across all three
reported segments, with digital/eCommerce channels now contributing the
majority of that growth -- but the evidence base provides no margin,
free-cash-flow, or leverage figures to independently verify the financial
quality behind that growth, and Walmart's own filing names thin retail
margins as a structural, unresolved risk.

**Expanded narrative:** Walmart's scale ($713.2B FY2026 revenue, 280
million weekly customers, 10,900+ stores) [WMT-C01, WMT-C02] provides
purchasing, logistics, and pricing advantages difficult for most
competitors to replicate, and the EDLP/EDLC model is a stated,
self-reinforcing strategy tying customer price leadership to cost
discipline [WMT-C04]. The most recently reported quarter shows real
comparable-sales growth in all three segments, with eCommerce contributing
a majority of Walmart U.S.'s gain and a meaningful share of Sam's Club's
[WMT-C06, WMT-C08] -- concrete evidence that the stated strategy of
expanding higher-margin ecosystem businesses (advertising, marketplace,
health/wellness, financial services) [WMT-C05] is translating into
measurable growth, not merely a stated aspiration. At the same time,
Walmart International's 18% net-sales growth included $2.3 billion of
favorable currency impact [WMT-C07] -- a reminder that headline growth
figures at this scale can be meaningfully currency-driven, and the evidence
bundle provides no margin, free-cash-flow, dividend, buyback, or leverage
figures for Walmart anywhere, leaving this record's financial-quality
assessment less complete than for companies in this corpus with fuller
disclosed profitability and balance-sheet detail.

**Conditions required for the thesis to remain valid** (qualitative,
observable -- no arbitrary numerical threshold):
- Comparable-sales growth continues across Walmart U.S., Walmart
  International, and Sam's Club U.S., or at minimum does not reverse
  sharply
- eCommerce/digital-channel contribution to comparable sales continues at
  a meaningful level, evidencing continued digital-monetization traction
- Ecosystem-business expansion (advertising, marketplace, health/wellness,
  financial services) continues, consistent with the company's stated
  strategy
- International growth is not disproportionately dependent on favorable
  currency movements over a normalized period

**Thesis-weakening conditions** (analytical judgment, presented for
review -- observable and qualitative, deliberately without a specific
numerical trigger):
- A sustained reversal of comparable-sales growth in any of the three
  reported segments
- A sustained decline in eCommerce's contribution to comparable sales,
  suggesting digital-monetization traction is stalling
- Evidence that Walmart International's growth is becoming increasingly
  currency-dependent rather than organic
- Any disclosed evidence of margin compression, were such evidence to
  become available in a future review (not assessable from this evidence
  base's current content)

   bundle does not break out the profitability of these ecosystem

and principal acceptance -- AI-assisted research and drafting do not
themselves constitute approval. Walmart shows genuine scale (280 million
weekly customers, 10,900+ stores, $713.2B FY2026 revenue) and continued

## Capital priority and next-dollar considerations

**Business quality**, per the evidence above, shows a dominant-scale,
defensive consumer retailer with continued comparable-sales growth and
real digital/ecosystem-business monetization traction, offset by a
structurally thin-margin business model and an evidence base that lacks
any margin, cash-flow, or leverage figures to independently verify
financial quality.

**Capital priority is a separate question, and WMT's own current T2
placement (1.65% target) is itself a data point this record preserves as
historical policy, not evidence.** Whether the next investment dollar is
better spent on WMT than on another T2 name or another governed holding
entirely is not settled by this record: this record does not perform a
cross-holding capital-priority ranking, and no numerical score or
composite index is produced. **What can be said qualitatively:** WMT's
scale and demonstrated digital-growth traction are real, evidenced
strengths, but the absence of any margin, cash-flow, or leverage figure in
this evidence base is a materially larger gap than in several other
records in this corpus (e.g. this repository's CVX and WDC records, which
disclose debt or cash-flow figures directly), limiting how confidently
this record can compare WMT's financial risk to other governed holdings.
**What would be lost if WMT were absent:** the portfolio's only large-
scale, diversified discount/membership-retail exposure with a demonstrated
digital-advertising and marketplace growth vector.

**Factors that could support greater capital priority** include WMT's
scale, its continued comparable-sales growth, and its evidenced
eCommerce/ecosystem-business traction. **Factors favoring an alternative
use of capital** include the structurally thin retail-margin model, the
currency-dependent share of recent international growth, and the complete
absence of margin/cash-flow/leverage evidence in this record's evidence
base. A reviewer weighting WMT's scale and digital-growth traction more
heavily could reasonably favor greater priority; a reviewer weighting the
margin-evidence gap more heavily could reasonably favor less. **This
record does not resolve that question and does not rank WMT against any
other holding.**

Business quality and portfolio capital priority are separate judgments.
This record does not determine the governed target: WMT's current T2
tier and 1.65% target remain binding, this Intelligence record does not
change them, and a final next-dollar decision requires portfolio-wide
comparison and principal approval.
