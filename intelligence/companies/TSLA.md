## Summary

Tesla produced strong Q2 2026 volume and revenue growth, but operating margin
compressed to 1.4%, quarterly free cash flow was negative, and 2026 capital
spending is expected to exceed $25B. The balance sheet is liquid, but current
market value depends heavily on autonomy, robotics, and earnings outcomes that
are not yet evidenced at the scale embedded in the nearest issuer-defined
milestone framework.

The repository's accepted gate refers to a “milestone framework” without
identifying its thresholds. Tesla's SEC-filed 2025 CEO performance award is the
closest direct issuer framework because it measures vehicle scale, paid FSD
subscriptions, delivered robots, commercially operating Robotaxis, and
adjusted EBITDA. That mapping is analyst interpretation, not a claim that the
award was the gate author's intended framework. On either a literal reading or
this closest-framework reading, the gate cannot clear.

## Business evidence

Vehicle deliveries reached a quarterly record and energy storage deployment
grew materially. Services and other revenue grew faster than the product
segments. These scale results are real, but aggregate gross margin declined to
16.8%, operating margin was only 1.4%, and heavy investment drove negative Q2
free cash flow.

Tesla reported Robotaxi service in seven major metropolitan areas. Its release
described the Bay Area service as supervised with a safety driver and listed
Austin, Dallas, Houston, Miami, Orlando, and Tampa as ramping unsupervised.
Tesla did not disclose a fleet count. Geographic availability and early
service operation therefore do not demonstrate one million Robotaxis in
commercial operation.

Optimus production lines were under construction or being installed. Tesla
said production was expected to start soon and that initial builds would be
used for its Academy and training. No delivered-bot count was disclosed.

## Issuer milestone framework

The SEC-filed CEO performance award contains 12 tranches. Each tranche requires
a market-capitalization milestone and one operational milestone. Operational
milestones include 20M cumulative Tesla vehicles delivered, 10M active FSD
subscriptions, 1M bots delivered, 1M Robotaxis in commercial operation, and
adjusted-EBITDA thresholds beginning at $50B and rising to $400B.

As of June 30, Tesla identified only the 20M-vehicle milestone as probable over
the award term for accounting purposes; probable does not mean achieved.
Tesla reported 9.7M cumulative deliveries and 1.48M active FSD subscriptions,
did not report a delivered-bot or commercial-Robotaxi fleet count, and did not
report any operational milestone as achieved.

Trailing-four-quarter adjusted EBITDA through Q2 2026 was $15.322B, calculated
from reported quarterly adjusted EBITDA of $4.227B, $4.154B, $3.668B, and
$3.273B. It is well below the first $50B threshold. This is a scale reference,
not a prediction of award vesting.

## Compensation and dilution

The award covers 423.7M performance-based restricted shares. Tesla reported
$9.82B of unrecognized stock-based compensation for the milestone considered
probable and $105.82B–$120.37B associated with milestones not considered
probable. Q2 stock-based compensation was $1.151B versus $635M a year earlier;
the Q2 SG&A increase included a $283M increase in stock-based compensation,
primarily related to the award. Tesla separately reported $267M of Q2 expense
for the award. Award economics, dilution, and related-party capital allocation
are material to any future per-share valuation.

## Facts, management adjustments, and interpretation

Revenue, GAAP results, cash flows, balance-sheet amounts, operating counts,
program descriptions, award terms, and accounting probability disclosures are
issuer-reported. Adjusted EBITDA is a management-defined non-GAAP measure.
Free cash flow, net liquidity, trailing sums, and market multiples are analyst
calculations. Mapping the award to the repository gate and concluding that the
gate is unmet are analyst judgments.
