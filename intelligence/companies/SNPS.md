## Business summary

Synopsys operates two segments post-Ansys: **Design Automation** (EDA
software -- silicon design/verification, now including Ansys's
multiphysics simulation products, digital/custom/FPGA IC design, and
manufacturing software) and **Design IP** (interface, foundation,
security, and embedded-processor IP; IP subsystems; IP implementation
services, serving mobile, automotive, digital-home, IoT, and AI/
data-center markets). [SNPS-C05] FY2025 total revenue was **$7,054.2
million**, +15% YoY (record). Headcount grew ~40% to ~28,000 employees
post-Ansys close. The company markets AI-driven EDA (Synopsys.ai) and,
since mid-2026, "agentic"/autonomous AI chip-design workflows.

## The Ansys acquisition

**Announced 2024-01-16; completed 2025-07-17** (China's final regulatory
approval came 2025-07-14; Ansys delisted from Nasdaq at market open
2025-07-17). [SNPS-R01] **Deal terms -- two distinct figures, not a single
number, corrected in this record (2026-08-05) following independent
review**: the **originally announced** merger agreement (2024-01-16) set
consideration at **0.3450 Synopsys shares plus $197.00 cash** per Ansys
share. Per the agreement's own anti-dilution/exchange-cap provision (if
stock consideration would have exceeded 19.9999% of Synopsys shares
outstanding pre-merger, the exchange ratio was reduced to the minimum
extent necessary with cash correspondingly increased to offset), the
**final consideration actually paid at closing** (2025-07-17) was
**0.3399 Synopsys shares plus $199.91 cash** per Ansys share -- a
provision this record's research indicates was triggered during the
transaction's progress toward closing. Enterprise value ~$35 billion at
announcement. This distinction (announced-vs-final terms) was not
previously stated in this record; an earlier draft cited only the final
figures without noting they differ from the originally announced terms,
which an independent reviewer flagged as a likely factual error. Both
figures are corroborated across multiple secondary sources (Yahoo
Finance, CNBC) and an SEC S-4/A filing and Ansys investor-relations FAQ
page were identified by search as the underlying primary documents, but
**neither was directly opened this session** (WebFetch remained blocked
on sec.gov on a fresh attempt) -- this correction rests on WebSearch's
own synthesis of those sources, not a direct primary-source read, and
should still be treated as unverified-against-primary pending a future
session with working SEC access.
**Financing**: ~$19 billion cash consideration funded via cash-on-hand
plus debt; Synopsys secured $16 billion in fully committed debt
financing (an $11.7B bridge commitment plus a $4.3B term-loan facility)
-- one source separately cites $14.3 billion of debt actually incurred
for the deal, a figure not reconciled against the $16B committed-
financing figure in this research. **Synergy targets**: $400 million cost
synergies by year 3, $400 million revenue synergies by year 4, combined
long-term target >$1 billion annually; management has also cited a
long-term 40%+ operating-margin goal.

**Integration cost**: a restructuring announced November 2025 cut
approximately 10% of the workforce (~2,000 employees), with pre-tax
charges of $300-350 million (severance plus site closures), the majority
of cuts landing in FY2026 and the process expected substantially
complete by end of FY2027. **Purchase accounting**: the post-close
balance sheet carries $26.9 billion goodwill and $11.9 billion intangible
assets -- together roughly 83% of total assets are non-physical, flagged
by secondary sources as a material impairment-risk exposure if synergies
underdeliver. Ansys intangibles amortization is running at approximately
$403.6 million per quarter (Q2 FY2026 figure) and is the primary driver
of the large GAAP/non-GAAP earnings gap (see Financial results below).
Divestitures tied to antitrust clearance included Synopsys's Optical
Solutions Group and Ansys's PowerArtist business (buyer/terms not found
this session).

**Nvidia partnership/investment** (2025-12-01, figures stated with more
confidence than the underlying single-source-type sourcing supports --
hedged here per independent review, matching this record's treatment of
comparably-sourced items elsewhere): Nvidia is reported to have invested
$2 billion in Synopsys common stock at $414.79/share (~2.6% of shares
outstanding), becoming Synopsys's 7th-largest shareholder per LSEG data
cited by Reuters/CNBC -- these specific mechanics (price, share count,
ranking) trace to that one data-provider/press pairing and were not
independently cross-corroborated against a second, differently-sourced
outlet this session. The multi-year partnership pivots compute-intensive
Synopsys applications to Nvidia CUDA-X/AI-physics/Omniverse, with joint
go-to-market -- framed by secondary commentary as tied to Ansys's
compute-intensive simulation workloads.

## Recent financial results

Fiscal year ends October 31.

**FY2025** (Q4 released 2025-12-10): Q4 revenue $2.255B; FY2025 total
$7.054B (+15% YoY, record). Q4 non-GAAP net income $543.1M / $2.90 per
share (vs. $3.40 Q4 FY2024); FY2025 non-GAAP net income $2.138B / $12.91
per share (vs. $13.20 FY2024) -- both down year-over-year on a per-share
basis despite record revenue, reflecting Ansys-related dilution/costs.

**Q1 FY2026** (released 2026-02-25): revenue $2.409B, +66.0% YoY (Ansys
now in the base), beat guidance high end; GAAP EPS $0.34; non-GAAP EPS
$3.77 (vs. $3.03 Q1 FY2025), beat estimate by ~5.9%; FY2026 revenue
guidance reiterated around $9.61B midpoint including ~$2.9B of Ansys
revenue.

**Q2 FY2026** (released 2026-05-27): revenue $2.276B (vs. $1.604B Q2
FY2025); GAAP EPS $0.09 (GAAP net income $17.1M); non-GAAP EPS $3.35
(non-GAAP net income $643.7M), beat a $3.15 estimate; **gross margin 83%
for the first time**, described as the strongest of eight quarters;
non-GAAP operating margin 39.5%; GAAP operating income fell ~68% YoY due
to the $403.6M Ansys intangibles amortization; FY2026 guidance raised to
$9.665B revenue midpoint, $14.76 non-GAAP EPS midpoint.

**The GAAP/non-GAAP gap is extreme and directly evidences the gate's
"GAAP normalization... unresolved" concern**: Q2 FY2026 GAAP net income
($17.1M) versus non-GAAP net income ($643.7M) is roughly a 38x gap,
driven almost entirely by the non-cash Ansys purchase-intangible
amortization described above.

**The September 2025 shock** (Q3 FY2025 report, historical context): the
stock fell approximately 35-36% in one day (from ~$604 to ~$388) after Q3
FY2025 revenue ($1.74B, missing $1.77B consensus) and a severe guidance
cut -- full-year non-GAAP EPS guidance cut from $15.11-15.19 to
$12.76-12.80; Q4 non-GAAP EPS guided to $2.76-2.80 versus a $4.54
consensus. Cause: Design IP segment revenue $425.9-428M, down ~8% YoY,
attributed to three cited drivers -- China export-control disruption, a
major foundry-customer shortfall (commentary points to Intel, unconfirmed
by primary source), and internally acknowledged roadmap/resource-
allocation misjudgments. This is the single largest specific financial-
credibility event in Synopsys's recent history and the proximate trigger
for the workforce reduction described above. The stock's 52-week range as
of 2026-08-03 was $366.00-$636.25, trading around $392 -- still well
below its pre-drop highs roughly eleven months later, despite two
subsequent beat-and-raise quarters.

**Securities litigation directly tied to this event**: *Kim v. Synopsys,
Inc.* (N.D. Cal.), a securities class action (class period 2024-12-04 to
2025-09-09) alleging Synopsys misrepresented Design IP business strength
while AI customers' demand for increasing customization was quietly
deteriorating IP unit economics, and that certain roadmap/resource
decisions were unlikely to yield their intended results. A consolidated
stockholder derivative action is stayed pending resolution of a motion to
dismiss in the class action. **Status: unresolved, ongoing as of this
research date.** A separate, thinly-evidenced data-breach/cybersecurity
matter is under investigation by plaintiff firms -- flagged as low-
confidence, single-source, not corroborated.

## Competitive position

The EDA market is estimated to be ~78%+ controlled by three players:
Synopsys (~31% share, 2024 estimate), Cadence Design Systems (~30%),
Siemens EDA/formerly Mentor Graphics (~13%, some estimates 10-15%) -- an
oligopolistic structure secondary commentary attributes to end-to-end
tool suites and deep, multi-year foundry-certification relationships.
Synopsys is characterized as leading in synthesis/simulation tools;
Cadence leads custom/analog layout; Siemens EDA leads physical
verification. Post-Ansys, Synopsys markets itself as offering "the first
vertically integrated device-to-system design stack," fusing chip-level
EDA with Ansys's CFD/thermal/mechanical multiphysics simulation -- a
differentiation claim relative to Cadence, which does not own comparable
simulation breadth. EDA tools generally are described in industry
commentary as deeply embedded in customer design flows with high
switching costs and multi-year foundry-qualification cycles -- standard
industry framing, not independently verified against a primary Synopsys
disclosure this session.

## Material risks

1. **GAAP/non-GAAP normalization** -- an extreme, structurally persistent
   gap (see Financial results above), directly matching the gate's own
   named concern.
2. **September 2025 credibility event and its aftermath** -- a Design IP
   segment revenue decline, a severe guidance cut, a ~35% one-day stock
   drop, and ongoing securities litigation, all still live/unresolved.
3. **China/export-control exposure** -- an acute, short-notice regulatory
   event in mid-2025 (BIS license requirement imposed then rescinded
   within about six weeks); China reported as roughly 10-12% of revenue
   in recent quarters (pre-Ansys base); recurring risk category for the
   EDA industry generally.
4. **Leverage** -- substantial notwithstanding paydown progress: total
   debt reduced from ~$13.5B (Q4 FY2025) to ~$10.0-10.8B (Q2 FY2026), but
   S&P's own leverage-decline forecast (1.8x FY2026E, 1.2x FY2027E) does
   not reach the company's own stated <1x net-leverage target within the
   forecast window found this session.
5. **Purchase valuation/impairment risk** -- $26.9B goodwill plus $11.9B
   intangibles (~83% of total assets) now on the balance sheet; analyst
   price-target cuts (~$550→~$480 per one source) cited "limited
   near-term visibility on Ansys-related profitability" and called
   FY2026 a "transition year."
6. **Activist-investor pressure** -- Elliott Investment Management
   disclosed a multi-billion-dollar stake around 2026-03-22/23, pushing
   for better software/services-portfolio monetization; outcome unknown.
7. **This session's own weak source access** -- no primary document
   directly opened (see disclosure above); several figures internally
   inconsistent across sources.

## Investment thesis (descriptive, no recommendation)

**Concise statement:** Synopsys's underlying business execution is
strong and improving (record revenue, first-ever 83% gross margin, major
new AI-industry partnerships), but the transformative Ansys acquisition
has left the company's reported earnings, leverage, and valuation
picture genuinely difficult to normalize as of this record's authoring
date -- exactly the condition the gate describes.

**Conditions that would support initiation**: a clean read from the
September 30, 2026 Investor Day on IP-business trajectory and long-term
margin roadmap; continued deleveraging toward the company's own <1x net-
leverage target; visible Ansys revenue-synergy realization; resolution
(dismissal or settlement) of the pending securities litigation; a stock
re-rating back toward pre-September-2025 levels reflecting market
resolution of these questions.

**Conditions that would argue for continued caution**: further Design IP
segment weakness; a material adverse Investor Day disclosure; leverage
plateauing rather than continuing to decline; an adverse ruling or
expensive settlement in the securities litigation; a China export-control
policy reversal recurrence.
